Avalon (also spelled Ynys Afallach) is the mystical island in Celtic mythology first mentioned by Geoffrey of Monmouth around 1139, where Arthur's sword was forged and where the wounded king was taken after the battle of Camlan to be healed.

## Key Facts

- Avalon derives its name from Welsh *afal* meaning "apple," with Geoffrey of Monmouth explaining it as *insula pomorum* "island of apples".
- The island corresponds to *Emain Ablach* "Emain of the apples," the otherworldly domain of the Irish sea divinity Manannán mac Lir on the Isle of Man.
- Nine sorceresses inhabit Avalon according to Geoffrey's *Vita Merlini*, led by Morgan (later Morgan le Fay).
- Pomponius Mela described similar nine virgin priestesses with healing and prophetic powers on the island of Sena off Brittany around 43 AD.
- Arthur is believed to live in Avalon and will return from there, making it a place of expected reincarnation and renewal.

## Q&A Sections

### What is the earliest mention of Avalon in literature?
Avalon first appears in Geoffrey of Monmouth's *Historia Regum Britanniae* (c. 1139) as *Insula Avallonis*, where Arthur's sword Caliburnus was forged and where Arthur was taken after the battle of Camlan. In Welsh versions of Geoffrey's work, it is called Ynys Afallach, maintaining the apple association through the Welsh word *afal*.

### How does Avalon relate to other Celtic otherworlds?
Avalon shares characteristics with Irish otherworld locations, particularly *Emain Ablach* "Emain of the apples," which was the blessed domain of Manannán mac Lir on the Isle of Man. The apple symbolism connects it to various Celtic otherworld traditions where sacred apple groves represent divine realms. Nine priestesses or sorceresses feature in both Avalon and earlier Celtic island sanctuaries, such as the nine virgin priestesses described by Pomponius Mela on the island of Sena off Brittany.

### What role does Morgan play in Avalon?
Morgan (later known as Morgan le Fay) is the chief of the nine sorceresses who inhabit Avalon according to Geoffrey's *Vita Merlini*. As Arthur's sister, she represents the connection between the mortal and fairy worlds, being "definitely one of the fairy women who, according to tradition, are inhabitants of the Celtic Otherworld sometimes known as Avalon". Her presence establishes Avalon as a realm of supernatural healing and otherworldly power.

### Why is Avalon associated with Arthur's return?
Avalon serves as Arthur's resting place where he awaits his prophesied return to aid the Britons. This belief reflects Celtic concepts of reincarnation and otherworld resurrection, where "Arthur lives now, and 'It is from there that the Britons of England and of France have for a long time awaited his coming'". The island represents a realm outside normal time where heroes can heal and prepare for their eventual return to the mortal world.

## In-Depth Overview

### The Apple Island Etymology
The name Avalon derives from Celtic words for apple, with Geoffrey of Monmouth explicitly glossing it as *insula pomorum* "island of apples" in his *Vita Merlini*. The Welsh form Ynys Afallach connects to *afal* "apple" and *afall* "apple trees," establishing the fundamental apple symbolism that characterizes this otherworld location. This apple association links Avalon to broader Celtic traditions of sacred groves and otherworldly orchards that appear throughout Irish and Welsh literature.

### The Nine Sorceresses Tradition
Geoffrey's *Vita Merlini* describes Avalon as inhabited by nine sorceresses, with Morgan as their chief. This sisterhood parallels earlier Celtic traditions, particularly the nine virgin priestesses described by Pomponius Mela around 43 AD on the island of Sena off westernmost Brittany. These priestesses possessed "the arts of healing, prophecy and power over the elements, and the ability to assume the form of animals", characteristics that would later be associated with the inhabitants of Avalon.

### Arthur's Otherworld Connections
Arthur's relationship with Avalon reflects his fundamental connection to the Celtic otherworld. His wife Gwenhwyvar's name means "white ghost" or "white phantom," connecting her to fairy-folk traditions. His sister Morgan is explicitly identified as one of the fairy women of the Celtic Otherworld. The sword Excalibur came from the Lady of the Lake, another supernatural benefactress who "watched over him" like the Irish Morrígu presided over Cú Chulainn.

### The Mystical Geography
Avalon is located "vaguely in the west" according to Geoffrey's description, following Celtic traditions that place otherworld islands in the western ocean. Welsh traditions speak of the *Gwerddonau Llion*, green fairy islands that "were supposed to be the abode of the souls of certain Druids" who occupied "this romantic sort of purgatorial paradise". These islands represent Celtic conceptions of otherworld realms existing parallel to the mortal world but accessible through special circumstances.

### Healing and Regeneration Themes
Avalon serves as a place of healing where Arthur's wounds from the battle of Camlan are tended. This healing function connects to broader Celtic concepts of otherworld regeneration, including the Cauldron of Re-birth that could resurrect slain warriors. The island represents a realm where normal limitations of mortality and time are suspended, allowing for recovery and eventual return.

## Primary Sources

***Historia Regum Britanniae*** *c. 1139*
*Geoffrey of Monmouth*
> Insula Avallonis (the Isle of Avalon) is first mentioned by Geoffrey of Monmouth in his Historia Regum Britanniae (c. 1139) as the place where Arthur's sword Caliburnus was forged, and then as the place where Arthur was taken after the battle of Camlan for his wounds to be tended.

***Vita Merlini (Life of Merlin)*** *c. 1150*
*Geoffrey of Monmouth*
> In Geoffrey's Vita Merlini, Insula Avallonis is explained as insula pomorum 'island of apples'. Vita Merlini's Insula Avallonis is located vaguely in the west and is inhabited by nine sorceresses, the chief of which is Morgan.

***De Chorographia (Geography)*** *c. 43 AD*
*Pomponius Mela*
> Writing c. ad 43 in his Geography, Pomponius Mela described a cult of nine virgin priestesses living on the island of Sena, probably Île-de-Sein off westernmost Brittany. These priestesses are described as having the arts of healing, prophecy and power over the elements, and the ability to assume the form of animals.

***Welsh Triads (Trioedd Ynys Prydein)*** *c. 13th century*
*various manuscripts*
> A mi ddysgoganaf dyddaw etwa Medrawt ac Arthur modur tyrfa, Camlann danner than difieu yna Namyn seith ni ddyrraith or gymmanfa. 'I prophesy that there shall come again, Medrawd and Arthur, ruler of hosts, to Camlan . . . on Thursday; only seven came from the engagement.'

***Brut y Brenhinedd (Welsh Historia)*** *12th century*
*Welsh translation of Geoffrey*
> In the Welsh versions of Historia Regum Britanniae (Brut y Brenhinedd), the place is called Ynys Afallach.

***Lai of Lanval*** *12th century*
*Marie de France*
> With her he goes away into Avalon, According to what the Briton tells us, Into an isle, which is very beautiful.

***Le Morte d'Arthur*** *c. 1470*
*Sir Thomas Malory*
> Yet some men say in many parts of England that King Arthur is not dead, but had by the will of our Lord Jesu into another place; and men say that he shall come again, and he shall win the holy cross.
