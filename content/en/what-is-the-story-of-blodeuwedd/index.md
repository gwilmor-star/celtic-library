Blodeuwedd (also spelled Blodeuedd) is a figure from Welsh mythology who was magically created from flowers by the sorcerers Math and Gwydion to be a wife for Lleu Llaw Gyffes, but who betrayed her husband and was transformed into an owl as punishment.

## Key Facts

- Blodeuwedd was fashioned from the flowers of oak, broom, and meadowsweet by Math son of Mathonwy and Gwydion.
- She reportedly betrayed her husband Lleu Llaw Gyffes by taking a lover and conspiring to kill Lleu, though the sources do not confirm all details of this conspiracy.
- As punishment for her betrayal, Gwydion transformed her into an owl, condemned to be shunned by other birds and never show her face in daylight.
- Her story forms a central part of the Fourth Branch of the Mabinogi.
- Her name changes from Blodeuedd ('flowers') to Blodeuwedd ('flower-face') to reflect her transformation into an owl.

## Q&A Sections

### How was Blodeuwedd created and why?

Blodeuwedd was magically created from flowers by the powerful sorcerers Math son of Mathonwy and Gwydion to serve as a wife for Lleu Llaw Gyffes. According to the Fourth Branch of the Mabinogi, they "took the flowers of the oak, and the flowers of the broom, and the flowers of the meadowsweet, and from those they conjured up the fairest and most beautiful maiden that anyone had ever seen". This extraordinary creation was reportedly necessary because Lleu was under a curse that prevented him from having a wife from any existing race of people, though the sources do not confirm the specific details of this curse.

### What was Blodeuwedd's betrayal of Lleu?

Blodeuwedd's betrayal reportedly involved taking a lover and conspiring to kill her husband Lleu, though the sources do not confirm the identity of the lover or specific details of the conspiracy. The story parallels the biblical tale of Samson and Delilah, as Blodeuwedd persuaded Lleu to reveal the secret conditions under which he could be killed. Through her deception, she learned the precise circumstances that would make Lleu vulnerable and arranged for an attack under those specific conditions.

### How was Blodeuwedd punished for her betrayal?

When Gwydion discovered Blodeuwedd's treachery, he pursued her and her maidens as they fled across the mountains. Her companions fell into a lake and drowned, but Blodeuwedd was caught by Gwydion, who declared: "I will not kill you. I will do worse. Namely, I will release you in the form of a bird. And because of the shame you have brought upon Lleu Llaw Gyffes, you will never dare show your face in daylight for fear of all the birds. And all the birds will be hostile towards you". Thus she was transformed into an owl, forever shunned by other birds.

### What is the significance of her name change?

The text notes that her name changes from Blodeuedd ('flowers') to Blodeuwedd ('flower-face') to reflect her transformation into an owl. This linguistic shift emphasizes the contrast between her original nature as a beautiful maiden created from flowers and her final form as an owl with a distinctive facial disc, highlighting the tragedy of her fall from grace.

## In-Depth Overview

### The Magical Creation

The story of Blodeuwedd begins with an act of extraordinary magic in the Fourth Branch of the Mabinogi. Math son of Mathonwy and his nephew Gwydion faced the challenge of finding a wife for Lleu Llaw Gyffes, who was reportedly under a curse that prevented him from marrying any woman of existing races, though the sources do not confirm the specific details of this curse. Their solution was to create a woman entirely from flowers, specifically "the flowers of the oak, and the flowers of the broom, and the flowers of the meadowsweet". This creation represents one of the most striking examples of transformation magic in Welsh literature, producing "the fairest and most beautiful maiden that anyone had ever seen."

### The Pattern of Betrayal

Blodeuwedd's story follows a familiar folkloric pattern that echoes the biblical tale of Samson and Delilah. Like Delilah, Blodeuwedd uses her intimate relationship with her husband to extract crucial information about his vulnerabilities. The text notes that she "persuades her husband to disclose the answers" to the riddle-like conditions that govern Lleu's mortality. This betrayal is not merely personal but represents a fundamental violation of the marriage bond and the trust placed in her by her creators.

### Geographic and Cultural Context

The tale is deeply rooted in Welsh topography, with specific locations playing important roles in the narrative. When Blodeuwedd flees after her betrayal is discovered, she and her maidens head for the mountains and cross "the river Cynfael" before reaching "a court that was on the mountain". The story provides onomastic explanations for various place names, including "Nantlleu" meaning the 'Valley of Lleu' and "Llech Gronw" meaning 'Gronw's Stone'. These geographic associations suggest that the tale may have originated as a way to explain local landmarks and place names.

### Literary and Mythological Significance

Blodeuwedd's transformation into an owl reportedly carries symbolic meaning within Celtic mythology, though the sources do not confirm specific interpretations of this symbolism. Gwydion's curse ensures that "all the birds will be hostile towards you" and that she will "never dare show your face in daylight", emphasizing her eternal exile from the natural world she was originally part of. Her story is said to be commemorated in the Welsh triads, though the sources do not confirm her inclusion as one of the "Three Disloyal Retinues".
