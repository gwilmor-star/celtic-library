Cú Chulainn is the legendary hero of the Ulster Cycle in Irish mythology, known for his supernatural strength and reportedly single-handed defense of Ulster against external threats. Born as Sétanta, he earned his heroic name meaning "Culann's hound" and became the central figure of medieval Irish heroic literature.

## Key Facts

- Cú Chulainn was born with the name Sétanta before earning his heroic title "Culann's hound".
- He is considered a warrior hero and demigod in the Ulster Cycle, believed to be an incarnation of the god Lugh.
- His mother was Deichtine, sister of King Conchobar mac Nessa of Ulster.
- He famously defended Ulster single-handedly against Connacht's forces in the epic Táin Bó Cúailnge.
- He was eventually killed and decapitated by Lugaid from Munster, then avenged by his comrade Conall Cernach.

## Q&A Sections

### What is the meaning and origin of the name Cú Chulainn?
His birth name Sétanta may be linked to the Setantii, a Celtic tribe on the west coast of Britain. The name Cú Chulainn translates as "Culann's hound," explained in tales where he replaced Culann's guard dog. While cú literally means "hound," it was also a figurative term for warrior in early Irish literature.

### What supernatural abilities did Cú Chulainn possess?
Cú Chulainn was described as a demigod with extraordinary combat abilities. The tales describe his supernatural birth and fantastic powers that set him apart from ordinary warriors. One tradition states his power was contained in his middle finger. His heroic biography follows typical patterns of fantastic birth scenarios found in Celtic mythology.

### What role does Cú Chulainn play in the Ulster Cycle?
Cú Chulainn serves as the central protagonist of the Ulster Cycle tales. His narratives focus on themes of societal integration and valor, contrasting with other heroes like Finn whose stories explore isolation and supernatural affiliation. The Ulster Cycle presents him as the defender of Ulster against external threats.
