Cú Chulainn is the legendary warrior hero and central protagonist of the Ulster Cycle of Irish mythology, renowned as the greatest champion of Ulster and famous for his supernatural battle-fury and tragic early death.

## Key Facts

- Cú Chulainn was born as Sétanta and earned his name "Hound of Culann" after killing the smith's guard dog and offering to replace it.
- He is believed to be the son of the god Lugh and the mortal Deichtine, sister of King Conchobar mac Nessa of Ulster.
- His most famous supernatural ability was the ríastrad or "warp spasm," a battle-fury that transformed him into a monstrous warrior.
- He served as Ulster's champion during the Táin Bó Cúailnge (Cattle Raid of Cooley), defending the province single-handedly while the other warriors were afflicted by a curse.
- He died young due to breaking his geasa (taboos), including being forced to eat dog meat, which sealed his fate.

## Q&A Sections

### What is the origin of Cú Chulainn's name?
Cú Chulainn's birth name was Sétanta. He received the name "Cú Chulainn" (meaning "Hound of Culann") after killing the guard dog belonging to the smith Culann and offering to serve as its replacement until a new hound could be trained. This episode is one of his most famous boyhood deeds and marks his transformation from child to hero.

### What supernatural abilities did Cú Chulainn possess?
Cú Chulainn's most distinctive supernatural ability was the ríastrad or "warp spasm," a battle-fury that physically transformed him into a terrifying warrior. He also possessed extraordinary physical attributes, including "seven pupils, four of them in his one eye, and three of them in the other" and "seven fingers on either hand, and seven toes on either of his two feet". His many gifts included "the gift of prudence until his warrior's flame appeared, the gift of feats, the gift of buanfach (a game like draughts), the gift of chess-playing, the gift of calculating, the gift of sooth-saying, the gift of discernment, the gift of beauty".

### How did Cú Chulainn die?
Cú Chulainn's death was caused by his breaking of geasa (supernatural taboos) placed upon him. His fate was sealed when he was forced to eat dog meat—one of his taboos—offered by an old crone, creating a conflict between his geas against eating dog and the powerful Irish taboo against refusing hospitality. Queen Medb conspired with various enemies, including Lugaid son of Cú Roí, to orchestrate his death through these supernatural constraints.

### What role did Cú Chulainn play in the Táin Bó Cúailnge?
During the Cattle Raid of Cooley, Cú Chulainn served as Ulster's sole defender while the other warriors were incapacitated by a curse. He fought a series of single combats at fords to delay the invading army led by Queen Medb and King Ailill of Connacht. His most tragic duel was against his foster-brother Fer Diad, whom he was forced to kill. This asymmetrical warfare, with one hero defending against an entire army, forms the central drama of Ireland's greatest epic.

## In-Depth Overview

### Divine Parentage and Birth
Cú Chulainn's supernatural nature stems from his divine parentage. According to the Ulster Cycle tradition, he was the son of Lugh of the Tuatha Dé Danann and the mortal woman Deichtine, who was sister to King Conchobar mac Nessa of Ulster. His birth involved magical circumstances, with Deichtine disappearing from the Ulster capital of Emain Macha and giving birth in the otherworld. The child was initially named Sétanta before acquiring his famous epithet.

### Training and Early Exploits
Cú Chulainn's martial education was extraordinary, taking him beyond Ulster to train with the warrior-woman Scathach in Scotland. Forgall the Wily, father of Cú Chulainn's beloved Emer, had suggested this training hoping the young hero would perish in the attempt. Under Scathach's tutelage, Cú Chulainn mastered numerous supernatural fighting techniques including "the apple-feat, the thunder-feat, the blade-feat, the supine-feat, and the spear-feat, the rope-feat, the body-feat, the cat's-feat, the salmon-feat of a chariot-chief, the throw of the staff, the whirl of a brave chariot-chief, the gae bulga (bag spear), the wheel-feat, the breath-feat, the hero's whoop".

### The Ríastrad and Battle-Fury
Cú Chulainn's most terrifying attribute was his ríastrad or warp spasm, a supernatural battle-fury that transformed him physically. During these episodes, he became "a monstrous thing, hideous and shapeless, unheard of". This transformation was both his greatest weapon and a source of concern for those around him, as it made him dangerous to friend and foe alike. The ríastrad represents the liminal nature of the hero, existing between human and monstrous, civilized and wild.

### Love and Marriage
The hero's romantic life was marked by both passion and tragedy. His pursuit of Emer, daughter of Forgall the Wily, required overcoming numerous obstacles including the dangerous training with Scathach. The text reveals that "Cu Chulainn had no wife at that time" and that the men of Ulster sought to find him a bride, "knowing that his rebirth would be of himself". His relationship with the otherworld woman Fann also caused significant turmoil, requiring druidical intervention to restore harmony.

### The Single Combat Tradition
Cú Chulainn's role as Ulster's champion was defined by single combat, particularly during the Táin. He fought numerous duels at fords, using the ancient Irish custom that allowed one warrior to hold up an entire army through individual challenges. His most emotionally devastating battle was against Fer Diad, his foster-brother and fellow student of Scathach. The text describes how "well-matched and alike was their manner of fight and of combat. Under the same instructress had they done skillful deeds of valor and arms, when learning the art with Scathach and with Uathach and with Aife".

### Supernatural Weapons and Techniques
The hero wielded several supernatural weapons, most notably the gae bulga. This weapon required elaborate preparation and caused unique wounds, as described in his combat with Fer Diad. His mastery of various supernatural feats set him apart from ordinary warriors, making him nearly invincible in single combat. These abilities were complemented by his exceptional physical attributes and divine heritage.

### Death and Legacy
Cú Chulainn's death was orchestrated through the manipulation of his geasa, the supernatural taboos that bound him. Queen Medb and his various enemies reportedly conspired to create situations where he would be forced to break these taboos, ultimately leading to his downfall, though the specific mechanisms are not detailed in the sources (sources limited). His death represented a significant moment in Celtic tradition, where even the mightiest champions are subject to fate and supernatural constraints.
