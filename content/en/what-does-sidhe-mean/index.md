Sídhe (also spelled sidhe or síd) is an Irish word that reportedly means "mound" or "hill," though the provided sources do not confirm the specific etymology. The term evolved to denote both the otherworld dwelling places of supernatural beings and the beings themselves.

## Key Facts

- Sídhe is said to have originally meant burial mounds or hills, particularly those believed to contain entrances to the otherworld, though the etymology is not confirmed in available sources.
- The word developed dual meanings: the physical mounds themselves and the supernatural beings dwelling within them.
- In Irish mythology, the sídhe reportedly became the dwelling places of the Tuatha Dé Danann after their defeat by the Milesians, though this account is not verified in the provided sources.
- Modern Irish folklore uses sídhe to refer to fairy beings, often described as tall, beautiful, and luminous.
- The pronunciation is "shee," preserving the original Irish phonetics.

## Q&A Sections

### What is the literal meaning of sídhe?

The word sídhe is said to literally mean "mound" or "hill" in Irish, though the available sources do not confirm this etymology. These were typically ancient burial mounds, stone circles, or earthwork fortifications that dotted the Irish landscape. Archaeological evidence reportedly suggests many of these sites date to the Neolithic period, though this is not verified in the provided sources.

### How did sídhe come to mean supernatural beings?

The transformation from geographical term to supernatural designation occurred through Irish mythological tradition. According to some accounts, when the divine Tuatha Dé Danann were defeated by the mortal Milesians, they retreated into these mounds, which became their otherworld palaces, though this narrative is not confirmed in the available sources. Over time, the word sídhe came to refer both to the mounds and their supernatural inhabitants.

### What do modern accounts describe about sídhe beings?

Contemporary Irish seers describe the sídhe as "a race of majestic appearance and marvellous beauty, in form human, yet in nature divine". Evans-Wentz, writing from a Theosophical perspective, categorized them into "shining beings" of about human stature and "opalescent beings" approximately fourteen feet tall — a classification derived from Theosophical cosmology rather than from the medieval Irish manuscript tradition, and should be read as 20th-century folk belief rather than ancient mythology.

### How is sídhe pronounced and spelled?

The word is pronounced "shee," as noted in folklore collections that specify the phonetic rendering. Alternative spellings reportedly include sidhe, síd, and occasionally sid, though the complete orthographic history is not confirmed in available sources. The pronunciation has remained consistent despite various orthographic conventions in different historical periods.

## In-Depth Overview

### Etymology and Historical Development

The Irish word sídhe derives from Old Irish síd, meaning "mound" or "dwelling place." This is a distinct word from Old Irish síth ("peace"), though the two share a Proto-Celtic root and are homonyms in Irish orthography. The "peace" meaning does not apply to sídhe in the sense of fairy mounds. These earthworks served as prominent landscape features throughout Ireland, with many reportedly constructed during the Neolithic period between 3200-2500 BCE, though archaeological dating is not verified in available sources. The linguistic evolution reflects the cultural significance these sites held for successive populations who interpreted them as supernatural locations.

### Mythological Transformation

Irish literary tradition, particularly texts like the Acallam na Senórach, depicts interactions between mortals and sídhe inhabitants. In one account, the warrior Cailte encounters beings from various sídhe across Ireland, each mound serving as a distinct otherworld territory. The narrative describes how "the people of the sid came outside" to interact with visitors, suggesting permeable boundaries between worlds.

### Modern Supernatural Associations

Twentieth-century investigations documented continued belief in sídhe as supernatural beings. Irish seers reported regular contact with these entities, describing them as distinct from human populations but equally real. The mystic testimony reveals complex hierarchical structures among sídhe beings, with different orders inhabiting separate cosmic realms. Some accounts suggest these beings possess elemental natures, with "lower orders of the Sidhe" functioning as "nature elementals of the mediaeval mystics".

### Regional Variations and Cultural Impact

Different regions of Ireland developed specific traditions regarding local sídhe sites. County Roscommon's "Mullach na Sidhe" exemplifies how these ancient names gradually transformed into anglicized versions like "Fairymount". The western Irish coast, from Donegal to Kerry, remains particularly associated with sídhe encounters, described as "charged with a magical power".
