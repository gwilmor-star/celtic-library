Manannán mac Lir is a major sea deity in Irish and broader Celtic mythology, reportedly with connections to oceanic waters and otherworldly realms. He appears prominently in medieval Irish literature as both a divine figure and, according to some sources, a historical merchant prince who was later mythologized.

## Key Facts
- Manannán mac Lir is primarily identified as a Celtic sea god with authority over oceanic waters and maritime domains.
- He possessed magical items including a self-navigating boat called "Scuaba Tuinne" (Wave-sweeper) and a horse named Aonbharr that could travel over land or sea.
- Medieval Irish texts present him as both a supernatural deity and explain him as a successful merchant who was later deified.
- He is closely associated with otherworldly realms and appears in tales involving journeys to supernatural lands.
- His name connects him to the Isle of Man, where he remains a significant figure in local tradition and folklore.

## Q&A Sections
### What role does Manannán mac Lir play in Celtic mythology?
Manannán mac Lir functions as a sea deity with dominion over oceanic waters and serves as a guide to otherworldly realms. He appears in Irish mythological texts as both a supernatural figure and, according to some medieval interpretations, as a historical merchant who was later mythologized by the Irish people. His role extends beyond simple sea-governance to include connections with the Celtic Otherworld and supernatural territories accessible through maritime journeys.

### What magical powers and possessions did Manannán mac Lir have?
According to the medieval Irish romance "Oidheadh Chloinne Tuireann," Manannán possessed several magical items. His most famous possessions included a self-navigating boat called "Scuaba Tuinne" or "Wave-sweeper" that could navigate without human guidance, and a horse named Aonbharr (Enbarr of the Flowing Mane) capable of traveling over both land and sea. Both items were reportedly loaned to the god Lugh Lamhfada, demonstrating Manannán's role in facilitating divine activities and otherworldly travel.

### How is Manannán mac Lir connected to the Isle of Man?
Manannán mac Lir maintains a strong traditional association with the Isle of Man, where he appears in local folklore and cultural traditions. The connection between the deity and the island reflects broader patterns of Celtic mythological geography, where supernatural figures become associated with specific landscapes and territories. In Manx tradition, he is known as "Manannan Beg mac y Leirr" and remains connected to local parliamentary traditions and cultural symbols.

### In which Irish texts does Manannán mac Lir appear?
Manannán mac Lir appears in various medieval Irish mythological texts and literature. He features in "Cormac's Glossary" (Sanas Cormaic), where he is described as inhabiting the Isle of Man and operating as both a divine figure and merchant. The romance "Oidheadh Chloinne Tuireann" provides detailed accounts of his magical possessions.
